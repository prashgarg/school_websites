A private school that posts its tuition
grows 4 percentage points faster.

We crawl and score the websites of 81,109 U.S. schools. What schools signal about religion, academic rigor, and cost transparency on their websites predicts which ones grow — and which ones close.

Key Findings

What we find

Six cross-cutting results, each with a concrete number. Click a topic to filter.

💰 Tuition transparency
+4.0 pp / SD

Posting your price predicts growth

A one-standard-deviation increase in tuition-transparency scores is associated with 4.0 percentage points faster annual enrollment growth (p < 0.001) — the largest single-topic effect in the data. Elementary schools drive the result; the effect is three times larger than for secondary schools.

🎓 College prep / rigor
+2.8 pp / SD

Academic rigor is the second-strongest growth signal

College-preparatory emphasis adds 2.8 percentage points of annual growth per standard deviation (p < 0.001). The effect holds across binary, tercile, and continuous representations, and survives dropping California or adding state × year interactions.

✝ Religious presence
r = −0.083***

Competition suppresses religious signaling

Each doubling of nearby private-school competitors is associated with a one-third standard-deviation decline in religious emphasis — the strongest competitive-suppression effect among all six topics. Religious schools serve bounded denominational markets; broader competition forces a broader signal.

✝ Religious presence
r = +0.072***

Religious identity tracks Republican geography

County Republican presidential vote share (2020) is the strongest geographic predictor of religious emphasis. STEM (r = −0.074***) and tuition transparency (r = −0.088***) show the opposite gradient. The partisan geography of school identity mirrors broader political sorting.

👨‍👩‍👧 Family partnership
r = +0.056***

Family partnership predicts public-school test scores

Among public schools, family-partnership emphasis on the website is the single strongest predictor of school-level SEDA achievement scores — even after controlling for socioeconomic composition and state. The website signal captures both a cultural orientation and real parent-communication infrastructure.

🔬 STEM emphasis
r = +0.080***

STEM rises with competition, tracks achievement

STEM emphasis is the only topic that rises both with local competition and with county test scores. Schools in denser markets signal STEM more — consistent with STEM programs drawing from broad secular markets willing to commute for a specialty program, unlike denominational schools.

What we measure

Six dimensions of school identity

From a 23-topic ontology, six topics show the clearest patterns and strongest outcome associations. Each is constructed from domain-specific regex patterns applied to crawled website text.

✝
Religious Presence

Faith mission, denominational affiliation, scripture, spiritual community. Retreats under competition; tracks conservative geography.

🌱
Character / Values

Character formation, leadership, integrity, moral education. Strongly associated with LinkedIn presence (r = +0.24).

👨‍👩‍👧
Family Partnership

Parent engagement, family-school partnership, volunteer programs. Strongest predictor of public-school achievement.

🎓
College Prep / Rigor

AP/IB courses, college counseling, test prep, placement outcomes. +2.8 pp annual enrollment growth per SD.

💰
Tuition Transparency

Tuition schedules, financial aid, scholarships, payment plans. +4.0 pp annual enrollment growth per SD — No. 1 predictor.

🔬
STEM Emphasis

Science, robotics, coding, maker spaces, engineering programs. Rises with competition and county achievement (r = +0.08).

About

The project

The School Websites Project builds a near-complete census of U.S. school websites through a multi-stage URL discovery pipeline, crawls them at scale, and scores website text using a structured topic ontology. We then link those scores to administrative enrollment and closure data to ask: does what schools say on their websites predict what happens to them?

The answer is yes. The website is not just a brochure — it is a strategic signal that encodes real positioning decisions, and families respond to it.
