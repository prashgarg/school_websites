What Do Private School Websites

We crawl and analyze the websites of every private and public school in the United States — uncovering how schools signal their identity, values, and academic priorities to prospective families.

Key Findings

What the data reveal

Across six focal topics scored from school website text, we find striking geographic patterns and significant associations with enrollment growth and school closure.

Tuition Transparency
+4.0 p.p.

Transparency predicts enrollment growth

Schools with higher tuition-transparency scores on their website grow enrollment significantly faster — about 4 percentage points per standard deviation, controlling for state and school characteristics.

Religious Presence
+7.2 pp

Religion tracks Republican geography

Religious emphasis on school websites correlates strongly with county-level Republican vote share (partial r = +0.072***). The partisan gradient is largest for religious content and reversed for STEM and college-prep.

College Prep / Rigor
+2.8 p.p.

Academic rigor signals growth

College-prep emphasis is the second-strongest predictor of enrollment growth. Schools in competitive markets (more private-school competitors within 10 km) show stronger STEM and character signals.

Competition
−0.083

Competition reshapes religious identity

A one-unit increase in log competitor count within 10 km is associated with a −0.083 partial correlation with religious emphasis — schools in more competitive markets de-emphasize religious identity on their websites.

Family Partnership
+0.056

Family engagement matters most in public schools

Among public schools, family-partnership emphasis is the single strongest predictor of school-level SEDA achievement scores (partial r = +0.056***), controlling for county demographics.

Grade Span
K–8 vs. 9–12

Elementary schools respond differently

The enrollment-growth effect of tuition transparency is concentrated among elementary schools. Combined K–12 schools show the largest variation in closure risk, driven by tuition-transparency and character-values signaling.

Six dimensions of school identity

Religious Presence

Signals faith-based identity, denominational affiliation, scripture references, and spiritual community values. Highest in the South and Midwest.

🌱

Character / Values

Character development, moral education, leadership, respect, integrity, and social-emotional learning. Associated with LinkedIn and YouTube social media presence.

👨‍👩‍👧

Family Partnership

Parent engagement, family-school collaboration, volunteer programs, and communication infrastructure. Strongest predictor of public-school SEDA achievement.

🎓

College Prep / Rigor

AP courses, college counseling, Ivy League placements, test-prep, and academic challenge. Associated with enrollment growth and competitive markets.

💰

Tuition Transparency

Visible tuition schedules, financial aid information, payment plans, and scholarship details. The strongest enrollment predictor in the data.

🔬

STEM Emphasis

Science, technology, engineering, math programs, robotics, coding, and maker culture. Positively associated with competition and county achievement.

About

The project

The School Websites Project is an academic research effort to understand how U.S. schools strategically present themselves to prospective families through their websites. We build a near-complete database of school websites through a multi-stage URL discovery pipeline, then crawl and classify website content using a structured topic ontology.

We find that website content is systematically related to enrollment growth, closure risk, competitive environments, geographic context, and student achievement — suggesting that school websites are both mirrors of educational strategy and signals that shape market outcomes.

Data coverage: PSS (Private School Survey, NCES) and CCD (Common Core of Data, NCES) provide the school universe. We successfully crawl websites for 81,109 of 145,569 U.S. schools (55.7%), including 94.9% of public schools and 40.5% of private schools.
