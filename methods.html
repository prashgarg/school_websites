Methodology

How We Measure School Website Content

A four-stage pipeline: school universe construction → URL discovery → web crawl → topic classification. Each stage is described below.

Stage 1 — School Universe

We begin with administrative data from the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), combining two primary sources.

1

Private School Survey (PSS)

The 2021–22 PSS covers 32,996 private schools in the continental U.S. We retain all schools with a valid NCES ID. The PSS provides school name, address, affiliation (Catholic, Non-Catholic religious, Nonsectarian), grade span, enrollment, and geography.

2

Common Core of Data (CCD)

The 2021–22 CCD covers 90,000+ public schools, including charter schools, magnet schools, and traditional public schools. We merge with local education agency (LEA) data to obtain district-level URLs as fallback seeds.

3

Merged spine

We combine PSS and CCD into a unified school spine of 145,569 schools, deduplicating on NCES school ID. The spine stores geographic coordinates (lat/lon from PSS), state, county FIPS, enrollment, and grade span.

Stage 2 — URL Discovery

Identifying the correct official website for 145,000+ schools requires a multi-stage pipeline combining multiple data sources. We achieve 81.6% URL coverage before crawling.

1

Administrative sources

URLs scraped directly from PSS and CCD records where reported. Covers approximately 60% of public schools and 25% of private schools in the initial pass.

2

District-sibling inference

For public schools without URLs, we infer the likely URL pattern from the district domain and school name (e.g., sub-directory or subdomain). This adds ~7,000 schools at near-zero cost.

3

OpenStreetMap & WikiData

OSM Overpass queries and WikiData SPARQL retrieve URLs for ~4,000 additional schools, primarily rural private schools and charter networks.

4

Catholic school networks

Diocese-level directories and the National Catholic Educational Association provide URLs for ~3,500 Catholic schools not otherwise covered.

5

IRS Form 990 data

For private schools organized as 501(c)(3) nonprofits, IRS 990 filings (via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer) provide website URLs for ~700 additional schools.

6

Wayback Machine CDX

Internet Archive CDX API provides historical URL records for ~5,000 schools whose current sites are no longer live but whose archived content remains accessible.

Coverage: After all URL-discovery stages, we have verified URLs for 118,753 / 145,569 schools (81.6%). Public-school coverage is 94.9%; private-school coverage is 40.5%. Remaining gaps are concentrated in small rural private schools and schools that lack a dedicated web presence.

Stage 3 — Web Crawl

We crawl each school's website using a custom Python crawler (Playwright + requests) deployed across a fleet of virtual machines.

Crawl design

For each school, we crawl up to 20 unique pages beginning from the seed URL. Pages are deduplicated within each school's domain; external links and PDF files are excluded from the page-content corpus (though PDF presence is noted). The crawler follows links only within the school's own domain (or its known subdomains), preventing contamination from linked third-party sites.

The crawl collects: page URL, page title, full visible text, HTML structural features (presence of forms, iframes, structured data), and metadata about social media links. We record the crawl date and HTTP status for reproducibility. The full corpus consists of 421,663 pages across 80,620 schools.

Crawl infrastructure

The national crawl was executed in May 2026 across five cloud VMs (shards 0–4), each handling a non-overlapping subset of the school spine. Total crawl time: approximately 48 hours. We cap crawl depth at 3 hops from the homepage to avoid deep-linking into student portals and administrative systems.

Exclusions

  • Schools whose domain returned HTTP 4xx/5xx for all attempts
  • Schools behind Cloudflare or similar bot-detection at crawl time
  • Schools whose URL resolved to a district homepage (identified by URL pattern mismatch with school name)
  • PDF-only sites (site serves only PDF documents, no HTML text)

Stage 4 — Topic Classification

We classify website text using a structured regex ontology of 23 topics, producing school-level prevalence and prominence scores.

Ontology construction

We develop a 23-topic ontology covering the main dimensions along which private schools differentiate their public identity. Topics are organized into thematic families: academic programming, faith and values, community and family, financial accessibility, and student support. Each topic is operationalized as a set of regular expressions capturing the vocabulary schools use when discussing that topic.

For example, the tuition transparency topic matches phrases like "annual tuition", "financial aid application", "payment plan", "scholarship available", "tuition schedule", and related variants. The religious presence topic matches denominational vocabulary, scriptural references, faith-community language, and mission-statement phrases common to religious schools.

Scoring

For each school-page pair, we count the number of regex matches per topic. The school-level topic score is the mean count per page, averaged across all unique pages crawled for that school. This averaging controls for variation in the number of pages crawled and ensures comparability across schools of different sizes.

We also construct a binary prevalence indicator (whether any page of the school's site mentions the topic at all) and a topic-page count (number of pages mentioning the topic). All three representations are available in the replication data.

Six focal topics

From the full 23-topic ontology, we highlight six topics that are most clearly defined, have high discriminant validity (low inter-topic correlation), and appear in prior literature on school choice and educational positioning.

Religious Presence
Faith mission, denominational affiliation, scripture, spiritual community. Highest in the South and Appalachian states.
Character / Values
Character education, leadership, integrity, moral development, social-emotional learning.
Family Partnership
Parent engagement, family-school partnership, volunteer programs, community involvement.
College Prep / Rigor
AP/IB courses, college counseling, Ivy League outcomes, academic challenge, test preparation.
Tuition Transparency
Visible tuition schedules, financial aid, scholarships, payment plans, cost information.
STEM Emphasis
Science, technology, engineering, math, robotics, coding, maker spaces, innovation labs.

Outcome and context data

E

Enrollment panels

PSS enrollment data for six waves (2007–08 through 2021–22) form an unbalanced panel. We compute log enrollment growth between consecutive observations as the outcome variable for growth regressions.

C

Closure data

School closure is identified via state DOE records for six states (CA, IL, NY, OH, PA, WI) covering 2018–2025. A school is coded as closed if it appears in earlier PSS waves but not in a subsequent confirmation check.

G

Geographic context

County-level covariates: 2020 presidential vote share (MIT Election Data and Science Lab), religiosity (ARDA), county FIPS (NCES SEDA file). State fixed effects are included in all regressions.

A

Achievement data

Stanford Education Data Archive (SEDA) v4.1 provides school- and county-level average test score estimates for public schools. County-level SEDA scores are used to contextualize private-school topic scores.
