Selling School: How Private School Websites Signal Identity, Rigor, and Transparency

Prashant Garg  ·  [Coauthor TBD]  ·  Working paper, 2026

Abstract

We construct a near-complete database of U.S. private school websites — crawling 14,384 private school sites and classifying content across 23 topics using a structured regex ontology. We document wide variation in how schools present themselves along six focal dimensions: religious identity, character and values, family partnership, college-preparatory rigor, tuition transparency, and STEM emphasis.

Schools with higher tuition-transparency scores grow enrollment 4.0 percentage points faster per standard deviation (p < 0.001), and college-prep emphasis predicts 2.8 percentage-point faster growth. In more competitive markets, schools shift away from religious emphasis (partial r = −0.083) toward STEM and character signals. Tuition transparency is positively associated with closure risk among K–12 schools, consistent with a "last resort" transparency model. Religious emphasis on websites tracks county Republican vote share (r = +0.072***) and is negatively correlated with all other topics at the county level.

These results suggest that school websites are not mere brochures — they are strategic signals in a competitive market that predict real enrollment and survival outcomes.

Selected results

Enrollment Growth
+4.0 pp / SD

Each standard deviation of tuition-transparency score is associated with 4.0 percentage points faster annual enrollment growth (cluster-robust OLS, state FE, n ≈ 50k school-years).

Competition
r = −0.083***

More competitors within 10 km (log count) is associated with reduced religious emphasis — a one-unit increase in log competition corresponds to roughly one-third of a within-state SD.

Geography
r = +0.072***

Religious emphasis correlates with Republican county vote share; STEM (r = −0.074***) and tuition transparency (r = −0.088***) are negatively correlated, revealing a clear partisan gradient.

Social Media
r = +0.239***

LinkedIn presence correlates most strongly with character/values scores. TikTok presence is negatively correlated with religious emphasis (r = −0.015*).
