What Schools Tell Families: Website Signals in U.S. K–12 School Markets

Ben Arold · Prashant Garg

Abstract

The U.S. K–12 market enrolls 50 million students across 130,000 schools with no required disclosure of price or program. We measure what 81,109 public, private, and charter schools tell families on their websites, using a fully replicable layer of keyword, structural, and transparency features extracted from 940,000 deduplicated pages. Three findings. First, every market-facing content dimension responds positively to local same-sector competitor density except religious identity, which is flat across 1–30 km on the private side and consistent with affiliation-anchored signaling. Second, on the private side a website mention of financial aid is associated with 1.77 percentage points faster annual enrollment growth and roughly 41 percent lower closure odds; academic-rigor and tuition-transparency content carry the same direction. Third, the academic-rigor coefficient on growth flips sign in the public sector, ruling out generic website-quality effects in favour of sector-specific sorting on observable content.
